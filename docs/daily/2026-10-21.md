## What that means — and what it does not

The sample policy has only three rules:
`git status → allow`, `deployctl release → prompt`, `rm -rf → forbidden`.

- The direct forms work exactly as written: `git status` allows,
  `deployctl release production` prompts, `rm -rf /tmp/x` is forbidden.
- Ordinary variants of the *forbidden* command mostly get no decision:
  `rm -fr`, `rm --recursive --force`, and wrapped forms `bash -lc`,
  `sh -c`, `env`, `sudo`, `timeout 5`, `xargs`, `./rm`, `command rm`
  all returned unknown at the rules layer.
- Flag dependence is real and documented: `/bin/rm -rf` is unknown with
  default flags, and forbidden with `--resolve-host-executables`.
- Chained commands passed as literal tokens are decided by the leading
  segment only (F-11a/F-12a) — the later segment receives no rules-layer
  decision in that invocation.

Unknown is NOT a bypass, evasion, or a hole. It means only: no rules-layer
decision. The runtime outcome is governed by the approval policy and the
sandbox under the pinned configuration. That distinction (Revision 2)
is the whole credibility of this work and must stay in every public version.

Honest scope note for any public post: this is a synthetic probe corpus
deliberately weighted toward wrappers and variants to map coverage — it is
NOT a measurement of real-world policies, and 54% must never be quoted
without that qualifier.
